OBS Stopped Recording Unexpectedly? How to Fix It & Never Lose Footage Again

By Nikhil Agarwal • Published July 2026 • 6 min read

You just finished an amazing 2-hour gaming session. You hit your hotkey to stop the recording, and you realize... it stopped on its own 15 minutes in. You just lost hours of incredible footage. It’s the worst feeling in content creation. Here is exactly why OBS does this, and how to fix it.

🚀 The Quick Answer

OBS silently stops recording primarily for three reasons: 1) You ran out of hard drive space, 2) Your CPU/GPU encoder overloaded and crashed, or 3) The file path you chose is corrupted or disconnected (like an external USB drive). Check your storage first.

Why Did OBS Stop Recording Randomly? (The Top 3 Causes)

When I first started creating content, I lost an entire let's play series because OBS crashed silently in the background. After digging through dozens of OBS log files, I found that the software is highly sensitive to system bottlenecks. Here are the three most common culprits you need to check right now.

1. You Ran Out of Disk Space (The Most Common Culprit)

If you are recording at high bitrates (e.g., 50,000 Kbps for 4K video), your file sizes will balloon rapidly. OBS writes video data directly to your drive. The exact millisecond your drive hits 100% capacity, OBS will instantly abort the recording to prevent system-wide corruption.

  • The Fix: Open OBS, go to Settings > Output > Recording. Check your "Recording Path". Open Windows Explorer and ensure that specific drive has at least 50GB of free space before you start a session.

2. Encoding Overloaded (GPU/CPU Bottleneck)

This usually happens right in the middle of a heavy gaming moment—a massive explosion or a fast-paced team fight. When the screen changes rapidly, your encoder (usually NVIDIA NVENC or x264) has to work twice as hard. If the encoder fails to keep up and the queue fills up, OBS will drop the recording entirely rather than freezing your PC.

  • The Fix: Lower your recording quality slightly. Go to Settings > Output > Recording. If your preset is on "Max Quality", lower it to "Quality" or "Performance". Ensure you are running OBS as Administrator, which gives it priority over your game for GPU resources.

3. Disconnected File Paths or External Drives

Many creators record directly to an external USB hard drive or a network-attached storage (NAS) drive. If the USB cable wiggles even slightly, or your network dips for a second, OBS loses the connection to the file and instantly stops the recording.

  • The Fix: Always record to an internal SSD (C: or D: drive). Once the recording is finished, move the file manually to your external drive for storage. Never record directly to USB.

The Real Problem: OBS Doesn't Tell You When It Fails

Fixing your disk space and encoder settings will solve 90% of your problems. However, here is the harsh reality: OBS will eventually crash again.

Windows updates break drivers. Games receive patches that suddenly hog 100% of your GPU. Hard drives fill up faster than you expect. The fundamental flaw in OBS Studio isn't that it crashes—it's that it fails silently in the background.

When you are playing a fullscreen game, you cannot see the tiny red dot in the corner of the OBS window on your second monitor. If OBS stops recording, you won't know until you finish playing and check the folder.
