What Are the Correct Twitch Emote Sizes?

When you upload a standard, non-animated emote to the Twitch dashboard using the "Manual Mode," you are legally required to provide the same image in three specific square dimensions:

  • 112x112 pixels: This is the largest size. Twitch uses this high-resolution version primarily for retina displays, larger screens, and when users click on the emote in the menu to preview it.
  • 56x56 pixels: This is the medium size. It is commonly used for standard web browser chat windows and some tablet interfaces.
  • 28x28 pixels: This is the smallest and most common size. This is how your emote will actually appear inline within the fast-moving Twitch chat stream.

Our Twitch Emote Resizer instantly generates all three of these sizes as transparent PNG files, perfectly formatted and ready to be uploaded directly to your creator dashboard.

Tips for Designing the Perfect Emote

Even the best Twitch Emote Resizer can't save bad artwork. Because emotes are ultimately viewed at a tiny 28x28 resolution, you must design with readability in mind. Here are some golden rules for creating emotes that pop:

  • Use Thick Outlines: Add a 1px or 2px thick black or white stroke around your emote. This ensures it stands out regardless of whether a viewer is using Twitch's Dark Mode or Light Mode.
  • Avoid High Detail: Do not include tiny text or intricate patterns. At 28x28 pixels, complex details blur into a single colored smudge. Keep it simple and expressive.
  • Maximize the Canvas: Make sure your artwork touches the very edges of the square canvas before you run it through our Twitch Emote Resizer. Dead space around the artwork just makes the final emote look even smaller in chat.
